So we talked about fundamentals, about resources/communities and colloboration,
but you may ask yourself:

**Now, how do I actually get a job?**

This lesson serves to answer this particular question.

You see, being good at your craft isn't the only thing that you need to get a job. It's a great start, but it's just that: **a start**

It might come as a shock to you, but there's actually people behind the recruiting/hiring etc.🙃

So you will need to (get to) know people in order to boost your chances of getting a job.

There's multiple ways you can approach this:

- Going to events like career fares, summits or conventions regarding your field
- Reach out to people directly via e-mail or hiring platforms like LinkedIn \- **more on that later!**
- Ask around in your social circle

Each approach comes with its own pros and cons to consider, but we will start step by step.

## Career fares, summits and events

The thing with these is that you will most likely have to invest at least a whole day to attend them and you might have to travel a little as well.

**But**, they can be a really cool way to get to know people in person and get a conversation started with the context the event provides!

## Reaching out directly via e-mail or hiring platforms

So this one is tricky because there's no apparent shared context like an event would provide, instead you'll have to search for one yourself.

This can be multiple things like _you went to the same school_ or _you have a shared hobby/interest_.

The thing is, you'll have more success with warm connections than with cold connections.

**What the heck are these?**

you may ask.

Glad you did. So... Warm connections are people that you have something in common with: maybe you went to the same school or you know them distantly through a friend or you both love cats or whatever really!

Cold connections on the other hand are people with which you don't really have something in common with. These connections will be much harder to maintain or even just use effectively.
